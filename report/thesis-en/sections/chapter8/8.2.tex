\section{Search Quality Evaluation}
\label{sec:8.4}

\subsection{Protocol}

\paragraph{Method.} The pipeline was run with the current configuration (YOLO11n at a confidence threshold of 0.1, ByteTrack, RaSa, every frame) on the labelled frames of the seven WILDTRACK cameras, 400 frames per camera, which produced a gallery of 1,526 tracks; each track was given, for evaluation only, the majority person among its matched labels. The evaluation set has 26 queries, each in three forms: a cropped image, an English description and a set of attributes. The identities were chosen among people seen on all seven cameras with at least 30 labelled boxes, and their descriptions and attributes were written by hand from three crops on different cameras using only the attribute vocabulary of the application; two identities walking next to a person dressed alike were left out. The observation used as the query image is removed from the gallery, and every other labelled observation of the same person is a correct result, 5 to 33 per query. \emph{Recall@$k$} is the fraction of queries with at least one correct result in the top $k$, with $k \in \{4, 8, 12, 16\}$, and \emph{MRR} is the mean reciprocal rank of the first correct result; text and attribute queries are tokenised with RaSa's own tokenizer (Section~\ref{sec:3.5.4}). With 26 queries one query is 3.8 points of recall, so Table~\ref{tab:recall} gives the 95\% Wilson interval of Recall@8. An earlier run of the first six queries at a confidence threshold of 0.25, on a gallery of 1,552 tracks, gave the same picture: Recall@8 of 1.0 by image and 0 by text and by attributes.

\paragraph{Person detection and tracking.} WILDTRACK labels people as positions on the ground plane and gives, for every fifth frame (400 per camera), the box of each labelled person in each camera view. A detection counts as correct when it overlaps a labelled box of the same frame with an IoU of at least 0.5, boxes being matched one to one in decreasing order of IoU; an unmatched detection is an extra box and an unmatched label a miss. At the confidence threshold of 0.1, the 2,800 labelled frames give 27,194 correct boxes, 35,602 extra boxes (2,278 of them second boxes on an already matched person) and 15,527 misses, a precision of 0.43 and a recall of 0.64; the earlier run at 0.25 gave 0.58 and 0.54. The precision is a lower bound rather than a measure of false detections: the labels only cover people inside the dataset's area of interest, and the cameras see many more, in the crowd on the steps behind the square in particular. In frame 400, for instance, about 19 of the 30 people visible on camera C1 and 3 of about 40 on camera C5 carry a label, and every correct detection of the others counts as an extra box. The recall is the meaningful figure: the lower threshold misses fewer labelled people, and the false and duplicate boxes it adds are mostly removed by tracking (Section~\ref{sec:3.2.5}). Of the 1,526 tracks, 491 match no labelled person (largely for the same reason), and 825 of the 1,639 labelled people are covered by at least one track; the others are mostly people seen only briefly or far from the camera.

\paragraph{Discussion.} Search by image performs well: 23 of the 26 queries have a correct result in the top 8, the first correct result is at rank 1 for 14 queries, and the three failures are at ranks 99, 109 and 553. Search by text and by attributes, in contrast, bring almost nothing into the top 16: one text query (at rank 12) and one attribute query (at rank 15). The first correct result lies at a median rank of 73 by text and 111 by attributes in the gallery of 1,526 tracks; with 5 to 33 correct tracks per query, a random order would place it at a median rank of about 150, so the text encoder separates very little on this data. Two causes are established below: the application searches with RaSa's contrastive vectors alone, while the model's accuracy relies on its cross-modal re-ranker, and the domain gap between CUHK-PEDES, on which RaSa was trained, and the person images cropped automatically from the full frames of WILDTRACK. Search by image is therefore the main form, while text and attribute search play a supporting role that needs careful visual assessment. This weakness of text search is the starting point of the research in Part~II (Chapter~\ref{chapter:r-overview}).

\paragraph{Qualitative check on the demonstration data.} On the demonstration data (Section~\ref{sec:7.2}), five query images were tried with the top 8 results and assessed visually: three queries had 8/8 correct results, one 7/8 and one 4/8; the correct results lay on 4 to 5 different cameras. This is only a qualitative check on a dataset different from the evaluation set and cannot be compared with the Recall values of Table~\ref{tab:recall}.

\subsection{Why Text Search Is Weak}

\paragraph{Pipeline check on the training domain.} To tell an implementation fault from the domain gap, the adapters that serve queries (image bytes through the image encoder, sentence through the text encoder, inner-product ranking) were run on 600 images of the CUHK-PEDES training split, the domain of the checkpoint, with their captions; the published test split is not redistributed, and the copy used carries the captions of a person on each of that person's images, so retrieval is scored per identity. Three things follow. Image-to-image retrieval of the same person is perfect (Recall@1 of 1.000 on 109 queries), so the image side is right. Text-to-image retrieval by the vectors alone, as the application searches, reaches Recall@1 of 0.035 and Recall@10 of 0.177, against 0.002 and 0.017 for a random order: well above chance, far below the published 76.5\%. Re-ranking the top 32 candidates with RaSa's cross-modal matching head, as the authors' evaluation code does over the top 128, raises Recall@1 to 0.650 on 40 of these queries over a 150-image gallery (contrastive vectors alone: 0.050), at about 0.2 seconds per candidate on the CPU. The published accuracy of RaSa therefore belongs to its re-ranker, which the application did not use; the contrastive vectors that the vector search compares are only its candidate generator. This, not an implementation fault, is the first reason text search is weak, before any domain gap. The check also found one difference from the authors' code: queries were tokenised with the standard BERT tokenizer, which closes a sentence with [SEP], while RaSa trains and evaluates without it; using RaSa's own tokenizer raised Recall@10 on this sample from 0.137 to 0.177 and is now the default, with the image vectors and the index unchanged.

\paragraph{Where the vector search fails.} Three further diagnostics on the same gallery and queries separate the crops from the encoder. First, crop size: the pipeline crops have a median height of 243 pixels (10th to 90th percentile 134 to 580); 1,100 of the 1,526 are shorter than the 384 pixels of the training crops and 122 shorter than 128, while the median aspect ratio of 0.38 is close to the training 0.33. Second, box tightness: cutting the same representative frames with the hand-drawn label box of the matched person instead of the detector box (possible for 947 tracks) moves text Recall@16 from 0.04 to 0.19 and the median rank of the first correct result from 73 to 67. Third, frame choice: a gallery made of the tallest label crop of each person on each camera, 1,439 crops of 266 people, gives text Recall@8 of 0.12 and Recall@16 of 0.19, with a median rank above 100. Tighter boxes and better frames help a little, but the vectors stay weak even on hand-picked crops, so what remains is the encoder's domain gap rather than detection or frame selection. The similarity scores say the same: the cosine of a text query with the 1,526 tracks has a mean of 0.19 and a standard deviation of 0.04, the correct tracks average 0.21 and the first result 0.30, so the right person stands less than half a standard deviation above the crowd, whereas on CUHK-PEDES a caption's own image stands a full standard deviation above the others (Section~\ref{sec:3.5.4}). Single-attribute queries make it visible: the top 16 of \emph{``A person wearing a red top''} contains no red top, and the top-16 lists of six such colour queries overlap with a mean Jaccard index of 0.30, 47 distinct tracks filling their 96 places, mostly close crops of dark clothing and hair. The vectors rank by how much a crop resembles a training crop rather than by what the sentence says, which is what the matching head corrects below.

\subsection{Two Ways to Improve Text Search}

\paragraph{Re-ranking the vector candidates.} Following that check, the matching head was added to the search flow as an optional stage (Section~\ref{sec:5.2.3}) and measured offline on the same gallery and queries: the vector search ranks the 1,526 tracks, the first $N$ candidates are re-scored by the matching head and returned in that order, and the rest keep their vector order. Table~\ref{tab:rerank} gives the result for $N$ from 32 to 128. The gain grows with $N$ because the vector ranking places the right person low: inside the first 16 candidates for 1 of the 26 text queries, inside the first 64 for 13 and inside the first 128 for 19. With $N = 128$, as in the authors' evaluation, text search reaches Recall@8 of 0.423 (95\% interval 0.26 to 0.61) and attribute search 0.154, from 0 for both. The cost is one fusion pass per candidate, 0.19 seconds on the CPU once the image tokens of the candidates are cached, so a query over 128 candidates takes about 17 seconds and over 64 about 9 seconds (Section~\ref{sec:8.7}). Attribute search gains less than free text: its template sentences name only garment types and colours, which many tracks share.

\paragraph{Another encoder on the same data.} The choice of RaSa over a general model (Section~\ref{sec:6.1.4}) was made without a measurement on the project's data, so the comparison was run afterwards with the same detection, tracking, representative frames and queries, replacing only the encoder with CLIP ViT-B/16 trained on LAION-2B~\cite{radford2021clip}, which has no re-ranker and costs about 0.15 seconds per crop on the CPU. Table~\ref{tab:encoders} gives the result. CLIP's vectors alone reach Recall@8 of 0.346 by text and 0.269 by attributes, where RaSa's vectors reach 0; this is close to RaSa with its re-ranker over 128 candidates (0.423 and 0.154) at about a hundredth of the query time. By image, however, CLIP reaches only 0.346 against RaSa's 0.885: the person-specific training of RaSa is what makes the image query work, and the general training of CLIP is what makes the text query work on camera crops. The larger CLIP ViT-L/14 (LAION-2B) gives Recall@8 of 0.423 by text, 0.346 by attributes and 0.423 by image. Prefixing the sentence with \emph{``a photo of''} changes CLIP's figures by at most one query. The conclusion for the design is that one shared vector space serves image queries well and text queries badly, and that the best text search on this data would come from a second vector per appearance from a CLIP-like encoder, or from RaSa's re-ranker made fast; Section~\ref{sec:12.3.1} takes this up.
