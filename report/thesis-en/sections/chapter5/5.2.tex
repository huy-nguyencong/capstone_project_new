\section{Main Processing Flows}
\label{sec:5.2}

\subsection{Camera Data Processing Flow}
\label{sec:5.2.1}

All analysis runs as queued \emph{processing jobs}, each tied to one camera and one source. For \textbf{automatic RTSP sessions}, when the queue is empty, the worker's scheduler picks the operational, AI-enabled RTSP camera that has waited longest and creates a session of 1,800 source frames (about 30 seconds at 60 frames per second); a camera whose last session failed is skipped for 5 minutes. The cameras are thus analysed in turn, with unanalysed gaps between two sessions of one camera (Section~\ref{sec:1.4}). For \textbf{uploaded video files}, the application server checks and stages the file, then creates the job.

\paragraph{Job life cycle.} Figure~\ref{fig:job-state} shows the states of a job. A \emph{Queued} job claimed by the worker becomes \emph{Running} with a 60-second lease, renewed during processing; if the worker dies and the lease expires, the job can be claimed again. For a video file job, transient errors are retried after a delay, up to three times; an RTSP session that fails ends at once, and the camera is processed again in a later session. A job ends as \emph{Succeeded}; as \emph{Failed} after an unrecoverable error or exhausted retries; or as \emph{Cancelled} by the Administrator, or when its camera is retired or its AI processing disabled.

\paragraph{Processing steps.} Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline-flow} shows the two phases of a job. In the \emph{per-frame loop}, each frame is read with its index and timestamp (Section~\ref{sec:3.7.4}), and one frame in $N$ goes through the Detector and Tracker. The result updates the \emph{track buffer}, which holds the active appearances and their representative frame candidates; when an appearance ends, its representative frame is chosen and kept in memory. When the source or session ends, the remaining appearances are closed and the job moves to the \emph{encode and write} phase: for each appearance, the person region is cropped in memory, widened by 5\% of the box on each side, encoded into a feature vector and written to the three stores (Section~\ref{sec:5.3.4}). Appearances are therefore searchable only after their job ends; for RTSP, a camera's data arrives session by session, every 1,800 frames. A job cancelled or expired during analysis writes nothing. Each appearance records the Detector-Tracker configuration, encoder version and sampling interval $N$ used.

\subsection{Representative Frame Selection}
\label{sec:5.2.2}

Each appearance stores \emph{one} full frame, used both for the vector and for display, so it should show the person as clearly as possible. The selector works as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Keep at most three candidates} per ongoing appearance, within 256~MB in total.
    \item \textbf{Hard filters.} A candidate needs a box of at least 24$\times$48 pixels, a sharp enough person region, fewer than two frame edges touched, and no abrupt shape change from the previous observation (area within 4 times, aspect ratio within 2.5 times), which usually signals a wrong person or occlusion.
    \item \textbf{The quality score} weights the person's size (0.30), sharpness (0.25), body completeness (0.20), stability (0.15) and closeness to the middle of the appearance (0.10), minus penalties for touching the frame edges or overlapping other people.
    \item \textbf{Choice at the end.} The best candidate passing the filters is chosen; if none passes, the best one is kept and the appearance is marked low quality rather than discarded.
\end{itemize}
The empirical criteria and weights are stored in a configuration file and give deterministic results.

\subsection{Search Flow}
\label{sec:5.2.3}

Figure~\ref{fig:search-sequence} shows the search flow.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{H4-tuan-tu-tim-kiem.png}
\caption{Sequence diagram of the search flow}
\label{fig:search-sequence}
\end{figure}

The web application sends the query with the camera filter, time range and $k$. The server then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{validates the input:} a JPEG or PNG image of at most 10~MB; attributes from the allowed list forming a valid combination; $k$ in $\{4, 8, 12, 16\}$;
    \item \textbf{encodes the query:} images with the image encoder, descriptions with the text encoder; attributes become an English sentence from a fixed template, such as \emph{``A woman wearing a red jacket and blue jeans, carrying a handbag.''};
    \item \textbf{determines the scope:} the area comes from the Operator's account, and the cameras are the selected ones or every operational camera of the area; others are rejected;
    \item \textbf{runs a pre-filtered vector search} in Milvus, checked against PostgreSQL and repeated with twice the number when valid results are missing, up to three rounds (Section~\ref{sec:3.6.4}).
\end{enumerate}
The list only carries descriptive information and scores; each image is a separate request, checked again and rendered from the representative frame (Section~\ref{sec:6.3.3}), so the list returns fast and no image skips the permission check.

\subsection{Case Management Flow}
\label{sec:5.2.4}

Figure~\ref{fig:case-sequence} shows how a result is saved into a new or existing case.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{H5-tuan-tu-vu-viec.png}
\caption{Sequence diagram of saving a result into a case}
\label{fig:case-sequence}
\end{figure}

The interface only sends the appearance identifier. The server checks that the appearance is ready and on an operational camera of the Operator's current area, and that the case is the Operator's own and open, then creates an entry with a \emph{snapshot} of the camera name, area and time, so the case stays correct if the camera is renamed or its frame is unavailable. Updates carry the case's version number, so concurrent changes are never silently overwritten. To close the case while saving, the interface reads the latest version number after the save and sends the status change; if that fails, the result is kept and the user is asked to close the case again. Images in a case are granted by the right over the case (Section~\ref{sec:4.6}).
