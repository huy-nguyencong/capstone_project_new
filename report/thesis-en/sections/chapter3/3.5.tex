\section{Joint Image-Text Representation Learning}
\label{sec:3.5}

\subsection{Joint Embedding Space and Contrastive Learning}
\label{sec:3.5.1}
\label{sec:3.5.2}

To compare a description with a person image, an image encoder and a text encoder produce vectors of the same dimension, trained so that an image and its description are close and non-matching pairs far apart, forming a \emph{joint embedding space}. Closeness is the cosine similarity
\begin{equation}
    s(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v}}{\lVert \mathbf{u} \rVert \, \lVert \mathbf{v} \rVert},
    \label{eq:cosine}
\end{equation}
which is the inner product $\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v}$ for unit-length (L2-normalised) vectors. Gallery images are encoded once at indexing, and a query only needs its own encoding (Section~\ref{sec:3.1.6}).

The common training method is \emph{contrastive learning}: in each batch of $B$ image-text pairs, the correct sentence of an image is the positive sample, the other $B-1$ sentences are negatives, and the loss
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_i = -\log \frac{\exp\left(s(\mathbf{v}_i, \mathbf{t}_i)/\tau\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^{B} \exp\left(s(\mathbf{v}_i, \mathbf{t}_j)/\tau\right)}
    \label{eq:contrastive}
\end{equation}
pushes the similarity to the positive well above the negatives, with $\tau$ a temperature parameter; the text-to-image direction is computed the same way. CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} applies this method to about 400 million image-text pairs from the Internet. ALBEF~\cite{li2021albef} first aligns the representations of the two modalities with contrastive learning, then fuses them in a multimodal encoder using cross-attention to match individual words with image regions in detail.

\subsection{The RaSa Model}
\label{sec:3.5.3}

General models such as CLIP and ALBEF struggle with small differences between similar people, such as the same jacket over trousers of another colour. RaSa (Relation and Sensitivity Aware representation learning)~\cite{bai2023rasa} is built on ALBEF and designed specifically for text-based person search, with a 12-layer transformer image encoder, a 6-layer text encoder and a 6-layer multimodal encoder. Its three key points are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Intra- and inter-modal contrastive learning.} Besides pairing images with sentences, images of the same person are pulled together, which is what lets the project use the same space for image queries.
    \item \textbf{Relation-aware learning.} A description is written for \emph{one} image and may not hold for the person's other images, so RaSa treats these two kinds of positive pairs differently.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity-aware learning.} The model learns to notice replaced key words, such as ``red shirt'' becoming ``blue shirt''.
\end{itemize}
RaSa can re-rank the top 128 results with its multimodal encoder, at a much higher cost than comparing vectors. On the CUHK-PEDES test set, RaSa reaches a Rank-1 of 76.51\% and an mAP of 69.38\%~\cite{bai2023rasa}.

\subsection{Use of RaSa in the System}
\label{sec:3.5.4}

The system uses RaSa weights pre-trained on CUHK-PEDES without retraining. The image encoder handles person crops at indexing and query images, resized to $384 \times 384$ pixels; the text encoder handles descriptions and attribute sentences. The 256-dimensional L2-normalised vectors let all three query forms use the same vectors by inner product, with one vector per track. Re-ranking only applies to text queries and costs one cross-modal forward pass per candidate, about 0.2 seconds on the CPU; the application offers it as an optional stage over the first candidates of the vector search, off by default (Section~\ref{sec:5.2.3}), because Section~\ref{sec:8.4} shows that it is where most of RaSa's accuracy lies but also that it costs about 17 seconds per query over 128 candidates on the target machine. Queries are tokenised with RaSa's own BERT tokenizer, which writes a sentence as [CLS] followed by the words with no closing [SEP], as the checkpoint was trained. The published checkpoint was saved from the training code, so it also holds a momentum copy of both encoders, the contrastive queues and the layers used only for re-ranking; the system loads only the two encoders and their projections, about 0.6~GB of the 1.9~GB in the file (Section~\ref{sec:6.2.1}).

Two caveats apply. RaSa was designed and evaluated for text search; using its image encoder for image queries is the project's choice, based on its intra-modal learning, not a published result. And CUHK-PEDES consists of tightly cropped people from five ReID datasets with a sentence per image~\cite{li2017cuhkpedes}, while the project's crops come automatically from full frames of a crowded area, with other viewpoints and frequent occlusion; this domain gap directly affects text and attribute queries (Section~\ref{sec:8.4}).
