\chapter{Experiments and Results}
\label{chapter:r-experiments}

\textit{This chapter presents the experimental setup on Market-1501, the results of the reasoning layer against the CLIP baseline with an analysis of what it rescues and what it loses, the ablations of its mechanisms, and the tooling that makes every number reproducible.}

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Dataset}
\label{sec:r-dataset}
The experiments use Market-1501~\cite{zheng2015market1501}, with the attribute
labels of the Market-1501\_\allowbreak Attribute project~\cite{lin2019market1501attr}:
1501 identities from six cameras on a university campus, with person boxes from an
automatic pedestrian detector rather than drawn by hand.

\paragraph{Why Market-1501, and how it stands in for a tracker.} The reasoning
layer needs several crops of the same person grouped together, so that the frames
of a track can agree or disagree about an attribute. Market-1501 labels every crop
with its identity, and grouping by identity gives each person observations under
different viewpoints, lighting, blur and occlusion, as a tracker would in the
deployed pipeline.

The groups span up to six cameras, so they act as an \emph{ideal} tracker that
never switches or merges identities and follows a person across cameras. Their
views differ more than the frames of a single-camera track, making disagreement
between readings, which the funnel reasons about, more frequent rather than less.
The third-party hand annotation of attributes~\cite{lin2019market1501attr} also
makes an attribute-level error analysis possible.

\paragraph{Why YOLO11n and ByteTrack are not in the experiment.} The experiment
isolates the search block: the encoder, the retrieval of the candidate pool and
the funnel behind it. Market-1501 already supplies the detected crops and the
identity grouping that detection and tracking would produce, so running YOLO11n and
ByteTrack would add nothing but their own errors. Every failure in
Section~\ref{sec:r-error} therefore belongs to retrieval or reasoning, at the cost
of not measuring upstream errors at all (Section~\ref{sec:r-limits}). Both remain
the chosen blocks of the deployed pipeline (Table~\ref{tab:r-choices}).

\paragraph{The 496-identity subset.} We keep only identities \emph{uniquely
identified} by their annotated attributes, that is, whose attribute vector no other
identity shares. This reduces 1501 identities to 496, the population behind every
rate in this part.

The task must be answerable from the description alone. If two people are described
identically, a query has two correct answers but the annotation records one, so
returning the other would be marked wrong although it is right. Removing them
removes noise the system cannot act on, but also makes the benchmark \emph{easier}
than the full 1501 identities, which matters whenever these numbers are compared
with published Market-1501 results.

\subsection{Models}
The experiment runs the search-block entries of Table~\ref{tab:r-choices}: CLIP
ViT-H/14 (laion2B) as base encoder, Milvus as vector database,
Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct as attribute describer and Gemini Flash-Lite 3.5 as
reasoning LLM at thinking level HIGH for every stage. No model is fine-tuned, every
number below is zero-shot, and sampling temperature is left at the provider
default, as advised for this model family.

crops is retrieved and its top 75 used; the coarse round keeps 25 and the final
round returns 10, with stage 2A split into three calls of 25.

\subsection{Baseline}
\paragraph{image-clip.} The baseline returns the top-10 \emph{crops} of the
75-candidate pool by cosine similarity, ungrouped, repeats kept: the same kind of
result as the funnel, from the same pool. Every table reports it.

\paragraph{A grouped baseline, considered and not reported.} An earlier sweep also
scored a \emph{track-naive} row assuming a perfect tracker: crops grouped by track,
each person scored by the maximum cosine over their crops blended with how many
surfaced ($0.7\,s_{\text{norm}} + 0.3\,c_{\text{norm}}$, normalised within the
query's pool). It is left out for two reasons. It returns ten \emph{people} rather
than ten \emph{crops}, so its R@10 has a different denominator of opportunity. And
on the 44-identity sweep where it was measured, it tied image-clip at every $k$ and
recovered no identity CLIP had missed, so this cheaper grouping alternative did not
help.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:r-metrics}
R@$k$ is the fraction of queries whose ground-truth identity appears in the
top-$k$. Alongside it, \code{cap@75} is how often the ground truth was in the
75-crop pool at all. No reranking can exceed it, so the gap from R@10 to
\code{cap@75} is what reasoning could still gain, and the gap from \code{cap@75} to
100\% belongs to retrieval. Without this ceiling, retrieval and reasoning failures
would look the same.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Main comparison}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{R@1} & \textbf{R@5} & \textbf{R@10}\\
\midrule
CLIP (image-clip)   & \textbf{26.0\%} & 29.0\%          & 33.0\%\\
2 stages            & 23.0\%          & \textbf{46.0\%} & 53.0\%\\
4 stages            & 15.0\%          & 38.0\%          & \textbf{56.0\%}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption[Main comparison on 100 identities]{100 identities out of the 496-identity subset
(Section~\ref{sec:r-dataset}), pool of 75 candidates, zero-shot, one descriptive
phrasing each. At $n=100$ a percentage point is one identity.}
\label{tab:r-main}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:r-main} gives three results.

First, the reasoning layer improves recall at depth: the four-stage funnel returns
the right person in the top 10 for 56 of 100 queries against CLIP's 33, 23 more
identities from reranking alone, without fine-tuning or changing retrieval. At R@5
its gain is 9 points (38.0\% against 29.0\%), while the two-stage variant gains 17
(46.0\% against 29.0\%).

Second, the funnel is worse at the first rank: CLIP leads at R@1 (26 against 23 and
15). This is not a rounding effect; the funnel is consistently better at placing
the right person somewhere in the top ten and worse at placing them first
(Section~\ref{sec:r-lost}).

Third, the refine round trades precision for depth. Two stages are better at R@1
and R@5, four at R@10: the second round lifts candidates the coarse round had
placed below the top 10, rescuing three more identities, but costs eight at R@1 and
eight at R@5. Which round should decide rank 1 remains open: the coarse ranker sees
75 candidates with sparse evidence, the refine ranker 25 with dense evidence.

\subsection{What the funnel changed: rescued and lost}
\label{sec:r-lost}
A recall figure hides two opposite effects. Table~\ref{tab:r-lost} separates them:
of the 33 identities in CLIP's top 10, how many survived, and of the 67 it missed,
how many the funnel recovered.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Record} & \textbf{n} & \textbf{Lost} & \textbf{Rescued} & \textbf{No pool}\\
\midrule
2 stages & 100 & 5/33 \ (15\%) & 25/67 \ (37\%) & 3/100\\
4 stages & 100 & 5/33 \ (15\%) & 28/67 \ (42\%) & 3/100\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption[Identities rescued and lost by the funnel]{Lost: inside CLIP's top 10, not in the funnel's. Rescued: outside CLIP's
top 10, inside the funnel's. No pool: the ground truth was not among the 75
retrieved crops at all, so no reranking could have returned it.}
\label{tab:r-lost}
\end{table}

The table reproduces Table~\ref{tab:r-main} exactly ($33 - 5 + 28 = 56$ for four
stages, $33 - 5 + 25 = 53$ for two), confirming it measures the same thing.

The rescue rate is the main result: 42\% of the identities CLIP missed in its top
ten are found by reasoning over attributes, on a pool CLIP itself produced. The loss
rate is the cost: about one in seven identities already found is pushed out. The
ceiling is close: only 3 of 100 ground truths were never retrieved, so 97 could
have been returned; 56 are, and the other 41 (41\% of the sample, not counting the
3 outside the pool) are lost inside the funnel. The remaining room is in the
ranking, not in retrieval.

Losses are the same for two and four stages (5), while rescues differ (25 against
28): the refine round adds rescues without adding losses at the top-10 boundary,
and its cost appears at R@1 and R@5.

\subsection{Is the ranker the bottleneck? A targeted re-run}
\label{sec:r-rerun}
The losses of Table~\ref{tab:r-lostwhy} are ranking failures by construction: the
right person was retrieved, described and ranked, but not high enough. They are
therefore where the question \emph{``is the small model the limitation?''} can be
asked directly.

The five were re-run with only the ranking stages switched to a larger model
(Gemini Flash 3.8 instead of Flash-Lite 3.5). Stages 0, 1 and 2A were
\emph{replayed} from the saved replies, so the new ranker read exactly the same
evidence; stages 3 and 4A ran again, since their inputs change with the coarse
ranking.

\centering

\textbf{Result}\\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption[The five lost identities re-ranked by a larger model]{The five lost identities re-ranked by a larger model at stages 2B and 4B
only. `Stage 2' and `Stage 4' read \emph{before} $\rightarrow$ \emph{after}.}
\label{tab:r-rerun}

Four of five are recovered, three at rank 1 of the refine round. Both coarse-cut
losses (candidates 2 and 5) pass the cut easily, from 27 and 30 to 1; the one still
wrong, candidate 4, is the one the original refine round demoted most. These losses
thus came from Flash-Lite's capacity at the ranking stages, not from the evidence:
the notes were sufficient, but the small model used them poorly.

One limitation is important: the five identities were chosen with the ground
truth, as exactly the queries the original run lost while CLIP had them. The
experiment shows whether the ranker is the bottleneck \emph{on these failures}, not
what the system would score with the larger model, which needs a full sweep; these
four recoveries must not be added to Table~\ref{tab:r-main}.

\subsection{Error analysis}
\label{sec:r-error}
Combining the three tables, the 100 identities divide as follows.

\begin{itemize}
\item 3 were never retrieved: outside the 75-crop pool, fixable only in retrieval.
\item 56 are returned, 28 of them only because the reasoning layer rescued them.
\item 5 were found by CLIP and lost by the funnel; four are recovered by a larger
      model reading the \emph{same} evidence (Section~\ref{sec:r-rerun}), so they
      are ranking, not evidence, failures.
\item 36 were missed by both. They are of two kinds, named but not yet counted
      separately: ambiguous attributes, where the describer is unsure (above all
      gender on small, rear-facing crops), and unread attributes, never written.
      Accessories are the clearest case: \at{carrying} is unread 28\% of the time,
      more often than its most common value \at{backpack} is read, so an
      accessory-based query favours candidates whose accessory \emph{happened} to be
      described.
\end{itemize}

The reasoning layer's own failures, the five lost identities, are the smallest and
most tractable category; the large remainder comes from upstream, from what the
index contains.

\section{Ablations and Design Decisions}
\label{sec:r-ablation}

Almost every mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:r-reasoning} was added after a measured
failure, and most can still be switched off. This section states what each changed
and how strong the evidence is, separating what was \emph{measured} from what is
only \emph{switchable}. Two limitations apply to all these numbers.

\paragraph{The sample behind a prompt measurement is a batch, not a sweep.} The
prompt-level numbers were collected while developing each stage, on batches of 25
to 75 candidates. They are large, categorical differences (19 to 0, 0 of 82), but
not recall measurements over 100 identities.

\paragraph{The model fixes a style per call.} Two back-to-back batches with the
same prompt produced 0 of 73 and 73 of 73 notes with a boundary citation: the
convention adopted at a call's first contested note holds for the rest of it. A/B
comparisons of prompt wording must therefore span several calls, or they may only
reflect this style lock.

\paragraph{Switchable but not yet measured.} The pipeline keeps one flag per
experiment: \code{LAB\_QUALITY\_POPULATION} (region quartiles over the candidate
crops rather than every frame of their tracks), \code{LAB\_PAIR\_PROFILE} (the
per-pair statistics block), \code{LAB\_FRAME\_AGREEMENT} (the \code{agrees} line
under each quoted frame), \code{LAB\_TYPICAL\_GUARD}, and \code{VIEW\_MIN\_UNUSUAL}
(how unusual a frame must be to print a \code{view} line). They are separate on
purpose: the quality-population flag \emph{corrects} an earlier error while the
others \emph{add} signal, and a result with all of them on would not show which one
caused it.

{\sloppy Three configuration settings are also untested: \code{CANDIDATES\_PER\_CALL}
(25, the source of the output truncation of Section~\ref{sec:r-engineering}),
\code{RANK\_KEEP\_EXTRA} (rows the ranker must write past the cut), and the
thinking level, HIGH at every stage and using roughly 70\% of a 2A call's output
budget.\par}

\paragraph{The ablation this part could not run.} The most informative experiment,
the whole sweep on the larger model, is limited by quota rather than design;
Section~\ref{sec:r-rerun} is the part that could be afforded.

\section{Evaluation Tooling and Reproducibility}

Two properties of the measurement setup changed results.

\paragraph{Records are rebuilt from replies, not trusted.} The sweep writes one JSON
record per configuration and one folder per query with every stage's rendered input
and reply. Two notebooks writing the same record file caused a \emph{lost update}:
each held the whole file in memory, so the later save silently dropped the other's
queries while the file still parsed and scored. The analysis notebook therefore
ignores the record files and rebuilds every ranking from the per-query folders,
mapping the ranker's labels back to crops by aligning the CLIP-similarity sequence
of the saved prompt with the retrieval cache, position by position. A query whose
alignment check fails is \emph{skipped and named}, never guessed, since attaching
labels to the wrong candidate list would produce a complete, plausible and wrong
record.

\paragraph{Every rate is reported with its denominator and its ceiling.} Queries
without a ground-truth track are counted and excluded, not silently dropped, since
a dropped query inflates every rate. \code{cap@75} is stored per query, so any
subset can recompute its own ceiling.

The supporting tools are: a per-identity viewer of the ground truth's CLIP rank and
its rank after each stage with the ranker's reason; a \emph{funnel-lost} report of
identities in CLIP's top 10 but not the funnel's (the source of
Tables~\ref{tab:r-lost} and~\ref{tab:r-lostwhy}); a coverage table over all
evaluated identities; and a work-queue notebook classifying every full-pool query
not yet evaluated as \emph{CLIP hit}, \emph{miss but inside the pool} (what
reranking can still gain) or \emph{miss outside the pool} (to be fixed in
retrieval).
