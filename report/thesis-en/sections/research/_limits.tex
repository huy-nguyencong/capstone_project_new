\label{sec:r-limits}
\paragraph{Model.} The reasoning LLM is Flash-Lite 3.5, chosen for its larger
free-tier allowance rather than its quality. Section~\ref{sec:r-rerun} confirms
the limitation, recovering four of five ranking failures by switching only the two
ranking calls to a larger model; a full sweep on that model, the most direct
improvement, has not been run.

\paragraph{Data.} Token limits restricted the evaluation to 100 of the 496
uniquely identifiable identities, so practical usability is not yet established,
and the subset is easier than the full 1501 identities
(Section~\ref{sec:r-dataset}). At $n=100$ a percentage point is one identity, and
the R@1 ordering of Table~\ref{tab:r-main} rests on differences of 3 and 8
identities. Extending the sweep needs no new work: the work-queue notebook already
picks the next identities, favouring those inside the pool but outside CLIP's top 10.

\paragraph{The blocks this part did not change.} Detection and tracking were left
as established defaults (Section~\ref{sec:r-choices}), yet their failures reach the
search block: a missed person has no crop, and a merged track holds two people.
This part measures neither. A tracker-error analysis over the same 100 identities
is the next measurement, and would move part of what Section~\ref{sec:r-error}
counts as a reasoning residue into an upstream category.

\paragraph{The refine round.} Four stages beat two at R@10 and lose at R@1 and R@5,
and three of the five losses in Table~\ref{tab:r-lostwhy} come from the refine
round. Whether stages 3 and 4 should return a full ranking, or only re-order the
candidates the coarse round was unsure about, is still open.

\paragraph{Rarity versus mismatch cost.} The rarity table tells the ranker what a
query value is worth when it separates candidates, but not the converse: a
\emph{binary} attribute such as gender is ``almost nothing'' by rarity, both values
being common, yet a gender mismatch is the strongest exclusion available. On one
query, 40 of 40 reasons led with the lower colour and gender appeared in 2, while
gender split the pool 44 to 31. This asymmetry remains to be handled.

\paragraph{Output truncation.} At 25 candidates per 2A call, replies occasionally
stop at the output limit mid-unit. The limit is detected afterwards from the API's
finish reason, and the two direct fixes, an explicit output cap or batches of 15,
have not been compared.

\paragraph{Single phrasing.} Each identity is queried with one descriptive
sentence, so robustness to paraphrase is not measured, although the pipeline
already supports several phrasings per identity.
