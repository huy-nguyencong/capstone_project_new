\section{Actors}
\label{sec:4.1}

The system has three actors, one per kind of user account: the \textbf{Administrator} (Admin), the \textbf{Operator} and the \textbf{Viewer}. All three share one login function; after login, the system only offers the functions and data of the account's role.

Two business concepts are used throughout the following chapters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Surveillance area:} a geographic scope such as a building or a public space. Each camera belongs to exactly one area, set when it is added and never changed. Areas are declared at deployment; the current version has no interface to manage them.
    \item \textbf{Case:} a record in which an Operator keeps the search results related to an incident, with a title, notes and a status of \emph{Open} or \emph{Closed}. A case belongs to its Operator, not to an area: the area only limits the search scope. Since an Operator's area can change, a case may contain results from several areas.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Administrator}
\label{sec:4.1.1}

The Administrator keeps the cameras and the AI processing running for the other actors, and is responsible for:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Accounts} of Operators and Viewers, including the area of each Operator.
    \item \textbf{Cameras} and their RTSP connections.
    \item \textbf{AI processing:} which cameras are analysed, and the person detector (Detector) and tracking algorithm (Tracker) used by the whole system.
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} the status of cameras and AI processing, checks of the AI components, and the audit log.
\end{itemize}
These technical rights do \emph{not} include business rights: the Administrator cannot search for people or see cases. The Administrator account is created at deployment; the interface can only create Operator and Viewer accounts.

\subsection{Operator}
\label{sec:4.1.2}

The Operator uses the main function of the system: searching for people in the analysed camera data, assessing each result in the context of its frame, and saving the matching results into cases until each case is closed.

Two scopes limit the Operator. The \emph{search scope} is the surveillance area of the account: only the operational cameras of that area are searched, and requests for other cameras or areas are rejected. The \emph{case scope} is ownership: each case has exactly one Operator, its creator, who cannot transfer it. The two scopes are independent: after moving to another area, an Operator still manages his or her old cases, while new searches run in the new area.

\subsection{Viewer}
\label{sec:4.1.3}

The Viewer is at management level, such as a head of department, and follows the results of the work without searching or configuring the system. The Viewer sees an overview of the cases of the whole system and every case with its saved results, read-only. Since cases are not tied to areas, the Viewer is not limited by area.

\subsection{Principles of Role Separation}
\label{sec:4.1.4}

The division follows two principles: \textbf{separating technical rights from business rights}, so that those who configure the system do not see search data and those who use the data cannot change the configuration; and \textbf{least privilege}, so that each actor only has the rights its work needs. The permission matrix of Section~\ref{sec:4.6} details these rights.

Cameras and the background AI processing are not actors: a camera is a data source configured by the Administrator, and the analysis runs automatically once AI processing is enabled.
