\section{Use Case Diagram}
\label{sec:4.4}

The functional requirements are organised into 15 use cases, shown with their actors and relations in Figure~\ref{fig:usecase} and listed with their purpose in Table~\ref{tab:usecases}.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{H1-use-case.png}
\caption{Use case diagram of the system}
\label{fig:usecase}
\end{figure}

The diagram follows four choices:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{An abstract \emph{User} actor.} Login (UC-01) and logout (UC-15) are linked to an abstract \emph{User} actor, which the three concrete actors generalise.
    \item \textbf{Division by role.} The Administrator has UC-02 to UC-08, the Operator UC-09 to UC-11 and the Viewer UC-12 to UC-14, with no use case shared.
    \item \textbf{Extend relations.} UC-10 extends UC-09 and UC-11 extends UC-10 (search, view a result, save it into a case); likewise UC-13 extends UC-12 and UC-14 extends UC-13. The extending use cases are also linked to their actor, who can start them alone, for example opening the cases without searching.
    \item \textbf{Login is a precondition} of every other use case, not a step inside them, so no «include» relation to UC-01 is drawn.
\end{itemize}

The automatic functions (Section~\ref{sec:4.2.5}) have no use case, since no actor triggers them; they run once AI processing is enabled in UC-04.
